Scoping Conversations

1. Understand the Business Context & Performance Gaps

Before discussing a solution, it's critical to understand the team's strategic goals, their key performance indicators (KPIs), and the specific business challenges they face.

Practical Tips & Questions:

  • Connect to Strategy: "What are the most important business goals for your team this quarter/year? How does this request support those goals?"
  • Identify Performance Gaps: "What specific metrics are you trying to improve (e.g., client satisfaction scores, sales conversion rates, loan processing times, compliance adherence)? Where are the biggest gaps between current performance and the target?"
  • Quantify the Impact: "What is the business impact of not addressing this issue? Can we quantify it in terms of revenue, risk, cost, or efficiency?"
  • Gather Evidence: "What data do you have that points to this being a skills or knowledge gap? (e.g., quality assurance reports, voice-of-customer feedback, performance data)."
  • Consider External Factors: "Are there any upcoming changes that might be impacting performance (e.g., new regulations, product launches, technology updates)?"
2. Define Business Outcomes & Learning Objectives

Move the conversation from a vague training request to a tangible business outcome. Clearly define what success looks like and what employees need to be able to do differently.

Practical Tips & Questions:

  • Focus on Behaviour: "After this initiative, what will employees be able to DO differently or better? What does success look like in action?"
  • Use the '5 Whys': Drill down from the business problem to uncover the root cause. (e.g., "The team isn't meeting its cross-sell target." Why? "They aren't identifying opportunities." Why? "They aren't confident asking the right discovery questions.")
  • Set Success Metrics: "How will we measure success? Let's link our measures back to the business KPIs we identified earlier. What's the target improvement?"
  • Explore Non-Training Factors: "Training is one tool, but what other factors might be influencing performance? Let's consider processes, systems, motivation, or role clarity."
3. Identify Constraints, Risks, and Dependencies

Every initiative has practical limitations. Identifying budget, timeline, technology, and resource constraints upfront is essential for designing a realistic and effective solution.

Practical Tips & Questions:

  • Clarify Resources: "What is the budget for this initiative? What is the ideal timeline for implementation?"
  • Learner Availability: "Realistically, how much time can employees be away from their daily tasks for learning activities? Are there peak business periods we need to avoid?"
  • SME & Stakeholder Commitment: "Who are the key subject matter experts (SMEs) we need input from? How much of their time can we secure? Who are the key stakeholders that need to be involved in reviews and sign-offs?"
  • Identify Dependencies: "Are there dependencies on other teams or projects (e.g., IT for system access, Compliance for content approval, Marketing for new product information)?"
  • Assess Risks: "What are the biggest risks to this project's success? (e.g., changing business priorities, lack of manager support, technology limitations)."
4. Clarify the Learner Audience & Scope of the Initiative

Defining clear boundaries prevents "scope creep" and ensures the learning solution is targeted to the right people with the right content.

Practical Tips & Questions:

  • Define the Audience Persona: "Who exactly is the target audience? (e.g., new personal bankers, experienced wealth advisors, team leaders). What is their current level of knowledge on this topic? What motivates them?"
  • Create an "In/Out" List: "What specific topics, skills, systems, or products will be covered? Equally important, what related topics are explicitly OUT of scope for this initiative?"
  • Discuss Modality: "Based on the need and audience, what type of solution might be most effective? (e.g., workshops, eLearning, job aids, a coaching framework, system simulations)."
  • Establish Change Control: "Once we agree on the scope, what will be the process if new topics or audiences need to be added?"
  • Agree on Roles: "Let's clarify roles and responsibilities. Who will be responsible for content provision, reviews, and final sign-off?"
